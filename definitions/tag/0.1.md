# Tag definitions 0.1

Status: complete working draft for prototyping and collaborator review. Version
1 is reserved for the first reviewed public release.

This document defines the human meaning of
`schemas/tag/0.1.schema.json`. A tag is a reusable controlled-vocabulary
record. A tag membership classifies an exact decoder version or circuit
revision; it does not itself establish scientific comparability.

## Namespace

The namespace states which independent discovery axis the tag belongs to.

- `algorithm` describes an ingredient or family of decoding algorithms and may
  be attached to decoder versions.
- `experiment` describes the purpose or protocol of a circuit, such as memory,
  stability, cultivation, or logical operation, and may be attached to circuit
  revisions.
- `code` describes the code construction represented by a circuit revision.

A tag is valid only in its declared namespace. Namespace is part of the tag's
meaning rather than a visual grouping.

## Scientific meaning

`description` is the normative short meaning of the tag. `label` is its display
name and `slug` is the stable URL/export identifier. Once the tag has been used
by published scientific content, its namespace, slug, and scientific meaning
are immutable. A genuinely different meaning requires a new tag record.

Tag memberships are non-exclusive: a decoder or circuit may legitimately have
several tags on the same axis. Tags support discovery and filtering. They do
not choose an evaluator, define a score, or determine whether two results can
be placed on one leaderboard.

## Aliases and parent tags

`aliases` records alternative names for the same tag. Aliases participate in
search and exact-name collision checks, but a membership always stores the
canonical tag identifier rather than the alias text.

`parent_tag_ids` records zero or more broader tags. Parent relationships form a
directed acyclic graph: a tag may have several parents and a parent may have
several children, but no path may return to its starting tag. Parentage does not
imply membership, equivalence, scientific comparability, or inheritance of any
other field. Parents may cross namespaces; the hierarchy is descriptive only.

## Curation status

`custom` means a community-created term which has not been adopted into the
admin-curated vocabulary. `official` means the admins have reviewed and adopted
the term. `deprecated` means the term should not be selected for new records.
Promotion from custom to official does not change the tag identity or its
scientific meaning.

`canonical_tag_id` identifies the same-namespace replacement after a merge or
deprecation. Historical memberships continue to name their original tag; user
interfaces may additionally resolve and display the canonical replacement.

## Governance and infrastructure

`status`, `canonical_tag_id`, curator fields, and curation timestamps are
governance metadata. `display_color` is admin-selected presentation metadata
for official tags and has no scientific meaning. Custom and deprecated tags
render neutrally even if historical colour metadata remains.

`id`, `schema`, `slug`, account references, and timestamps are identity and
infrastructure fields. Non-semantic spelling or wording corrections and
curation actions are audited, but do not create new decoder or circuit
revisions.
